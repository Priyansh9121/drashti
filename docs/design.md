# Drashti design rules

These are the rules for Drashti's operator UI, in its own look, **Diya** (Session 26g: the lamp's plum and flame, built from the design lab's "Diya, pushed further", `design-lab/diya/`). Later sessions follow them, and change this file when a rule changes. The tokens are in `src/renderer/src/styles/app.css`, the shared components in `src/renderer/src/ui/`, and the **component gallery** shows each one in every state: **Diagnostics > Component Gallery** (start Drashti with `DRASHTI_DIAGNOSTICS=1`), or `/gallery.html` under `pnpm dev`. Any new shared component goes into the gallery too. `tests/e2e/design.spec.ts` runs the accessibility checks on the gallery.

Write our own code. Design ideas may come from outside the workspace (Priyansh's All Skills collection, read as data), but nothing is copied into `app/` from there, from 21st.dev or from anywhere else. Brand matters (colours, the mark, the voice) were decided in the skills review's identity work and the design lab, and chosen by Priyansh on 15 Oct 2026 (PLAN.md §7, "Look and motion").

## 1. What the UI is for

Volunteers run a sabha with it in a dim hall, often under pressure. So:

- **Dark by default, lit by the lamp.** The window is plum-black and the panels a little lighter. Nothing bright is on screen unless it means something; the one decoration is the lamp's warm light falling from the mark (a gradient on a surface, never a filled shape, so never taken for a warning).
- **What is on the screens is never in doubt.** "Live" always looks the same (section 3).
- **Calm, and lit by the lamp.** One primary colour (the lamp's plum) on the one next thing to do, a few sizes, and the lamp's light as the only decoration. Movement only where something arrives or leaves (a dialog, a notice, a playlist row moving), each with its token, never on a show control (§3, "Motion"). Reduced motion turns it all off.
- **Familiar to operators coming from ProPresenter.** The library, playlists and media are on the left, the slides in the middle, and what's live and what's next on the right, with the layer clears along the bottom. The name "ProPresenter" never appears in the UI.

## 2. Colour

Colours are named for their **job**, never their hue (`bg-panel`, `text-muted`, `border-live`), so a light theme can come later by redefining the tokens. Don't use Tailwind's own palette (`red-500`, `amber-900` and so on) in new code.

| Token                                               | Job                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `ink`                                               | The window, behind and between panels                                      |
| `panel`, `panel-2`, `panel-3`                       | Panels; raised things (fields, cards, rows on hover); pressed and selected |
| `line`, `line-strong`                               | Dividers; dividers that must be seen                                       |
| `field`                                             | The edge of a control (text box, select, secondary button)                 |
| `fg`, `muted`, `faint`                              | Text; secondary text; the quietest text allowed                            |
| `accent`                                            | The focus ring, selection, links                                           |
| `primary-hi`, `primary`, `primary-lo`               | The primary: the lamp's plum, lit from above (one per screen)              |
| `primary-edge`                                      | A strong secondary's edge (Clear all in a show)                            |
| `live`                                              | On the screens now: always with a label                                    |
| `warning`, `warning-bg`, `warning-fg`               | Missing, can't play, needs a look                                          |
| `success`, `success-bg`, `success-fg`               | Connected, showing                                                         |
| `danger`, `danger-strong`, `danger-bg`, `danger-fg` | Removing and deleting; something failed                                    |
| `guide`, `selection`                                | The slide editor's snapping guides and what is chosen (never text)         |
| `--lamp-rgb`, `--lamp-header`, `--lamp-window` …    | The lamp's light and how strong it falls on each surface (§33)             |
| `--dur-*`, `--delay-*`, `--stagger-*`, `--ease-*`   | Motion: every duration, delay and easing (§3, "Motion")                    |

**One primary per screen, for the next thing to do** (Session 26g): in a show, **Next** under LIVE; on the first start, **Set up the screens**; with nothing chosen, step 1's **Import…**; in a warning, its next step (**Back up now**, a stopped screen's **Try again**); in a dialog, its own (Save, Finish; **Save changes** when typed work would be lost). Everything else in the window is a secondary (Clear all a strong one), and a primary that can't be pressed gives up its light. Next stays lit from the moment something is shown until nothing is, the last slide included, so a show never redraws it. `tests/e2e/next-thing.spec.ts` counts them.

**Diya** (Session 26g, from the design lab's "Diya, pushed further"): plum-black surfaces taken from the icon's tile, warm off-white text, and the state colours (`live`, `warning`, `success`, `danger`, ON AIR, REC, `guide`) exactly as they were. **The audience screens never take the look:** `body.output` (the outputs, the stream's picture, the stage display in a browser) keeps the colours and corners Drashti had before, and `tests/e2e/audience-pixels.spec.ts` (with `scripts/audience-pixels.mjs <commit>`) compares them picture for picture with an earlier build.

**Contrast** (WCAG 2.2 AA, measured by `src/main/contrast.test.ts` from `app.css` and `ui/Button.tsx` by the design lab's rules: every pair of its table, ratios compared unrounded, and the lamp's light at its brightest): `fg` 13.8 to 17.9:1 and `muted` 8.3 to 10.8:1 on the four surfaces, and `faint` at least 5.7:1 on every surface. White on `live` is 5.0:1, and on the primary 5.7:1 at its lightest (`primary-hi`) to 8.6:1 (`primary-lo`). Edges of controls are at least 3:1 where controls sit (`ink`, `panel`, `panel-2`): `field` 4.5 to 5.2:1, so a secondary button's edge is solid `field`, a danger button's solid `danger` (6.7:1 or more), and a strong secondary's `primary-edge` 3.2 to 3.7:1; the focus ring 11.3 to 13.1:1. **Colours that must never be confused:** the primary's three plums and `primary-edge` are at least 10 apart (CIEDE2000) from `live` and from ON AIR, in usual colour vision and in simulated protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia (Machado 2009); the closest is 12.3 (`primary-edge` and ON AIR, protanopia). The test fails below either rule and prints both tables. Text is never quieter than `faint`. Never put text on a surface with an opacity modifier (`text-fg/60`), because axe measures the mix. Run the accessibility checks after any colour change.

Group colours (Verse, Chorus…) come from the presentation, so they are shown as a swatch beside the group's name, never behind text. **The defaults** (`shared/group-colors.ts`, Session 26g): teal for Chorus, indigo for Verse, then orange, purple, amber and cyan; **no red**, because red sits too close to LIVE: every default is at least 10 (CIEDE2000) from `live` in usual and simulated colour vision (the closest, orange, 10.7 for a tritanope). A new group takes its name's colour, else the first default no other group has; groups already made, and colours a presentation brings from ProPresenter, keep their own.

## 3. "Live" is one colour plus a label

Anything on the screens now uses the `live` colour **and** says so in words: the `LiveBadge` ("LIVE"), "BLACK-OUT", "ON SCREENS", "Logo is on". Colour alone never carries the meaning (WCAG 1.4.1, and colour-blind volunteers):

- a live slide thumbnail has a `live` border **and** a LIVE label in its caption;
- a live row in a list (presentation, playlist item) has a `LiveBadge`;
- a layer clear is lit when its layer has something up: `live` edge and a dot, and the words "On screen" read out (`aria-label`);
- a button that is "on" (black-out, a prop shown) uses the `live` variant **and** changes its words ("Black-out is on", "Hide"). It says its state once: words that change, with no `aria-pressed` besides; or, where the words cannot change (the remote's half-width buttons), fixed words with `aria-pressed` and the state in words beside it (the preview's badge).

Nothing else uses `live`: errors use `danger` and warnings `warning`.

**Show controls change at once** (Session 25). Next, Back, Black-out, Logo, Clear all, Put it back, the layer clears, the Looks and masks buttons, the live thumbnail's border and caption, and Simple Mode's playlist rows take their new state in the same frame as the screens: no colour fade (`Button`'s `instant`), and the live slide is scrolled into view at once, never smoothly. Hover and focus on other controls may keep their short colour change. One name, **Black-out**, with its hyphen everywhere (a unit test checks), and one glyph, a filled screen (`BlackOut` in `ui/icons.ts`), in Pro Mode, Simple Mode and on the phones; Clear all is the eraser everywhere.

### Motion (Session 26g, "Rising")

Things arrive and leave; the show never waits for them. **Every duration, delay and easing is a token** in `app.css` (`--dur-*`, `--delay-*`, `--stagger-*`, `--ease-*`, the design lab's table), and nothing uses a number of its own (`src/main/motion.test.ts`); Tailwind's own `transition-colors` and the like take `--dur-hover` and `--ease-out`.

- **What moves:** a dialog rises 8 px (`--dur-dialog`, 220 ms) while the dim behind it only fades (`--dur-scrim`); a side sheet slides 16 px from its edge (`--dur-panel`); menus and "What is this?" pop from where they open (`--dur-menu`); a notice rises 6 px (`--dur-notice`); an empty place settles 14 px with one wisp of smoke from its lamp; a playlist row moving Up or Down slides to its place (`--dur-move`, 180 ms). Each says how it arrives with `data-motion` (`app.css`), and a dialog, menu or notice leaves in `--dur-exit` (about 70% of its entrance) as an inert copy that can't be focused, read out, clicked or found (`ui/motion.ts`), so the show's keys work the moment it closes.
- **What never moves:** Next, Back, Clear all, the clears, Black-out, Logo, Put it back, the Looks and masks, Simple Mode's buttons and rows, the thumbnails and the live picture, with their borders, badges and red glow (`data-show-control`, which no transition or animation reaches). Nothing bounces, springs or scales from nothing.
- **The window opens settled:** whatever is there when the window opens is simply there, with no arrival (an empty place shows its unlit lamp with no smoke); only what arrives afterwards moves (`useArrival` in `ui/motion.ts`). Opening lasts until the page and the window's first data have loaded (`holdOpening`: the library and the playlists, so an empty list that comes with them is simply there too) and the page is then first idle, or until the first key or click (`tests/e2e/motion.spec.ts`). Opening is when a computer without a graphics chip can least spare frames: Drashti starting, or starting again after a stop in a show while the screens are put back. The first start's welcome is the one arrival on opening.
- **Reduced motion:** with the computer set to reduce motion, nothing in the window moves at all, and no leaving copy is drawn (`tests/e2e/motion.spec.ts`). The audience screens' motion is the show's own (Session 27), and this rule leaves it as it was.

## 4. Type

- **The fonts are bundled Noto Sans, Noto Sans Gujarati and Noto Sans Devanagari** (`--font-sans`), so names in English, Gujarati and Hindi all look right offline. The weights are **400, 500 and 700**; use `font-normal`, `font-medium` and `font-bold`. `font-semibold` (600) is not bundled and renders as 700.
- Body text is 14 px (`text-sm`); small text 12 px (`text-xs`); the smallest is 11 px (`text-2xs`, for badges and panel titles only). Dialog titles are `text-lg`, page titles `text-2xl`. Simple Mode uses `text-xl` to `text-3xl`.
- Line height is at least 1.45 (the body default), even in one-line rows: Gujarati and Devanagari marks sit above and below the line, and a tight line cuts them off.
- Panel titles and section titles are 11 px bold capitals with wide tracking (`SectionTitle`, `Panel`). Nothing else is in capitals, and never a name someone typed (a playlist's header, a library's name): capitals do nothing to Gujarati or Hindi, and spaced letters break Devanagari's joining line, so typed names are shown as typed, 12 px bold at least (Session 25).
- Numbers that change (timers, counts) use `tabular-nums`.

## 5. Space, size and corners

- Spacing follows Tailwind's 4 px steps. Use 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8 (4 to 32 px). Gaps inside a control are 1 to 2, between controls 2 to 3, and between sections 4 to 6.
- **Control heights:** `sm` 28 px (panels), `md` 32 px (the default), `lg` 40 px (dialogs, live controls), `xl` 64 px and `xxl` 96 px (Simple Mode). Nothing clickable is smaller than 24 × 24 px (WCAG 2.5.8), and nothing on a phone's page smaller than 44 × 44 (§8).
- **Corners** (Diya: 6/8/12/18): `rounded-xs` (4 px) for key hints, `rounded-sm` (6 px) for badges, `rounded-md` (8 px) for controls and rows, `rounded-lg` (12 px) for cards and panels, `rounded-xl` (18 px) for dialogs and Simple Mode's buttons.
- **Depth, from light above:** cards (the live picture, the Screens cards) sit on `shadow-card`, and the slides' thumbnails (and a toggle's knob) on a crisp edge with no blur; overlays (menus, dialogs, tooltips, notices) float on `shadow-overlay`, a dark ring and a soft shadow. Every soft shadow is a blurred rectangle beneath its box: its spread takes in the box's corner (18 px for cards and overlays, the button's own for the ember, `--ember-spread`), so the light falls from above and the graphics chip draws every one in the same way. The live picture and the live slide glow red beneath, and that switches at once with the live border: the picture with `shadow-live`, and the live thumbnail, the media card and the practice show's live slide with a small `shadow-live-thumb`. The primary has its ember glow (`shadow-ember`). Nothing else has a shadow.
- **Diya costs a show nothing** (Session 26g, from the Performance check on CI's Mac, a virtual graphics chip, traced): the graphics chip works out a way of drawing (a pipeline) the first time each kind of drawing appears, and on a computer without a real one each costs 100–600 ms while every screen waits; the window's first drawing in a show needed 38 of them in Diya against 14 before it. So Diya keeps to a few: every soft shadow is a blurred rectangle (above); the lamp's light, the hairline under the header, an empty place's glow and the mark's halo are radial gradients only; the only other gradients are the primary's plum and the lamp lit on the first start (the mark beside a name is filled flat, each part in its gradient's middle colour, since the header is drawn again at every slide change); the thumbnails' edges are crisp; Next stays lit throughout a show (at the end it does what → does there: nothing more); and the window opens settled (§3, "Motion"), so its first data does not arrive with motion as a show begins. Nor does Diya add a layer of its own (`will-change`): each is put together again at every frame. `tests/e2e/diya.spec.ts` finds any blur round a rounded corner, any other gradient, any shape filled with a gradient and any layer of its own in the window, Simple Mode and the component gallery. Check any new kind of drawing in a place a show shows with the Performance workflow, paired against the commit before.

**Icons** are Lucide (ISC, `LICENSES/icons/`). Import them only from `ui/icons.ts`, and add a new one there (`BlackOut` is Lucide's rectangle, filled). Use 14 px in small controls, 16 px by default, and larger in Simple Mode. An icon is decoration (`aria-hidden`) unless it is the whole button, and then the button has a label.

**What sits over what** (z-index): the splitter 10, notices and the recovery banner 30, dialogs and the slide editor 40, menus 50, tooltips 60.

**Menus** show the focus ring on the choice that has the focus, give the focus back to what opened them when they close (unless a choice put it somewhere else, such as a name to type), and keep Esc to themselves: over a dialog or the slide editor, Esc closes only the menu. While one is open, the keyboard is in it (the menu itself when nothing in it can be chosen), and its keys (the arrows, Home, End, Page Up and Down, Space and Enter) never reach the show: Space chooses, as Enter does (Session 25).

## 7. Every place has its states

Every list, panel and dialog shows:

- **empty**: what goes here and how to add it (`EmptyState`);
- **loading**: when it waits for the main process (`Loading`), never a blank;
- **error**: what failed and what to do (`ErrorState` or a `Notice`), in plain words a volunteer understands, never a code, the system's own text or "Something went wrong" alone, and never "(s)" (`plural`). A file problem is said by `fileProblem` (`src/main/plain-errors.ts`: "The drive is full: free some space, or choose another drive."), and a phone is told "Drashti could not do that. Try again; if it happens again, tell the operator." The raw error goes to the log only; `src/main/messages.test.ts` scans the source for each of these (Session 25);
- **a window that fails as it draws** (Session 23): the operator's and a node's window show `ErrorState` with one primary button that reloads that window alone ("Reload the operator window"), and say the screens keep their picture (`WindowBoundary`). A screen (an output, the stream's picture) never shows words: it draws plain black (`SceneBoundary`) and tries again with the next change;
- **long names**: cut off neatly (`Truncate`) and shown in full on hover.

## 8. Keyboard and screen readers

- Everything works from the keyboard. The focus ring (`accent`, 2 px) is drawn by the base styles on every `:focus-visible` element; don't remove it. A component may add its own sign beside it (the Splitter's line thickens), never instead of it.
- **Dragging is never the only way** (Session 25). The chosen library row (a presentation, media file or Shastra passage) has **Add to playlist** on a line under it while a playlist is open, and every library row's menu has Add to "…": it goes after the playlist's chosen item, or at the end. The chosen playlist item has **Up** and **Down** on a line under it, and Alt+↑ ↓ on it; the keyboard stays on the moved item and the move is said ("Moved “…” up"). Each add and move, dragged or not, is one Undo step. The buttons go on their own line so the name keeps its room, and they hide while a drag is under way (a row that changes under the pointer as a drag starts makes Chromium cancel it).
- **"What is this?"** (Session 25): a small ? on each operator panel's heading (and on Playlists, the library, the slides and the live picture) opens two or three plain sentences under the heading, in the panel's own space, never over the show controls: what the panel is for and the one thing to do first, with Got it (Esc too) giving the keyboard back to the ?. The words are in `src/shared/panel-help.ts`, each following a passage of the operator's guide; `src/main/panel-help.test.ts` fails on a panel with no words or a passage the guide no longer has. Pro Mode only.
- **The keys sheet** (Session 25): Help › Keyboard Shortcuts…, or **?** when no field has the focus, in Pro Mode (Simple Mode keeps its key line). It is built from `KEYMAP`, grouped by what the operator is doing (`KEY_GROUPS`, every action once: a unit test checks), with each key as the computer writes it (`keyText`), and says the keys may change after the setup day. The menu item has no accelerator: ? is the page's key, so the guards for fields and dialogs hold.
- The show's keys (`src/shared/keymap.ts`) are ignored while a field has the focus or a dialog is open (`aria-modal`), and for any key a control has used itself (`defaultPrevented`): a control that handles a key calls `preventDefault()`, so the arrows between tabs, through a menu or on a splitter never also move the slide on the screens (Session 15).
- Lists you move through with the arrows use roving focus (Tabs, menus, radio groups through `ui/radio.ts`); long lists are virtual, so only the rows in view exist. A plain list of buttons (the playlists) is a list, not an ARIA tree: a tree takes the arrows, which belong to the show.
- A row with a right-click menu opens it from the keyboard too: Shift+F10 or the Menu key, handled by the row itself on every system (`isMenuKey` in `ui/Menu.tsx`; Chromium does it by itself only on Windows).
- **Landmarks.** The operator window is regions in the order it is laid out, and Tab goes through them in that order: the header, Playlists, Library, the slides, Live, Show controls (the layer bar) and the status bar. New panels go inside one of them. Only the splitters sit between regions.
- Every control has a name: its text, its `Field` label, or an `aria-label`. Icon buttons have their label as a tooltip too.
- Things that change on their own (what's live, import progress, notices) are in `aria-live` regions or have the status/alert role.
- **Larger text.** At 200% zoom (View, or the system's text size) every control can still be reached: below 960 × 540 CSS pixels the window scrolls rather than cutting columns off.
- **Reduced motion.** With the computer set to reduce motion, the interface's transitions and animations stop (`app.css`). The screens' own dissolves and the ticker are the show, not the interface, and keep moving.
- **Phones.** Every control on the phone and tablet pages is at least 44 × 44 CSS pixels, whatever its size class (a rule in `app.css` for `body.web`), and the pages never zoom on a double tap or reload on a pull (`touch-action`, `overscroll-behavior`, the stage display too). `tests/e2e/phone-targets.spec.ts` measures them at 390 × 844 in WebKit; a real iPhone and iPad are still owed. Elsewhere, 24 × 24 is the least (WCAG 2.5.8).
- `tests/e2e` runs axe-core (`tests/e2e/a11y.ts`) on the operator window, Simple Mode, each panel and the gallery, and fails on any serious or critical finding. `a11y-keyboard.spec.ts` runs a sabha by keyboard alone in both modes and checks the Tab order and focus ring; `a11y-display.spec.ts` checks 200% zoom, reduced motion, and the phone pages at 375 × 812.

## 9. Layout of the operator window

There are three columns between a header and a footer:

- **Left (Session 26a):** the **playlists** at the top, always there (never replaced by the open one): a heading, a labelled **+ New**, and rows that show **Rename** and **Delete** at their end on the chosen row and the row under the mouse (they keep their room, so no row changes size). Below a splitter, the **library** (Presentations and Media, the search box, then Import…, New… and, when shown, the filters).
- **Middle (Session 26a):** the **open playlist** above, the chosen item's **slides** below, with a remembered splitter between (`CentreColumn`). The playlist's header has its name (double-click renames), **Add files…**, **From a Link…**, its **⋯** menu and **×** (close). Its items are **cards** in a column, in order: an 80 × 45 picture (a presentation's first slide, a picture's or video's still, or the kind's icon on `panel-3`), the name, then a muted line with what it is and its length or slide count; a header is a plain bold label. The chosen card has Up and Down on a line under it; its trash keeps its place at the end of the card. With no playlist open, the slides have the whole middle. The slides are thumbnails in one grid, in play order, flowing on from one group to the next. Each thumbnail has its group's colour as a strip beside its number, and the first slide each time a group comes up carries the group's name. The thumbnail size (120 to 400 px, 170 at first) is remembered: at 170 three slides fit a row at 1280 × 720 and six at 1920 × 1080.
- **Right (Session 26a's order):** **LIVE** (the preview, headed so; no Next preview), with **Back** and **Next** under it (Session 26g: Next the one primary in a show, with its key; Back beside it, a third of the row; each does what its key does, at once), marker jumps under them, then **Audio** (a `PanelGroup` holding Music and the Arti as nested panels), the **Stage screen** (with Edit layout…), **Messages**, **Timers** (one compact row each: name, time, then icon buttons for Start or Pause, Reset and Show on the screens, and Edit; running timers first), **Props**, **Looks**, and **More tools** (a `PanelGroup` folded away to begin with: Macros, Masks and the Idle rotation, each after a `PanelNote` saying in one line what it is).
- **Along the bottom:** Clear all (a strong secondary since Session 26g, in its old place with F1; Put it back takes that place in the warning colour), the layer clears, Logo and black-out. Under them is the status bar: screens connected, the sound output, import progress and notices.

The columns are resized with `Splitter`s and the sizes are remembered on the computer (`ui/persist.ts`). Everything fits at 1280 × 720 with nothing cut off or overlapping, every control inside its column. Simple Mode is one screen with big buttons, and fits at 1280 × 720 too.

## 10. Simple Mode

Simple Mode (`src/renderer/src/simple/`) is for a volunteer who has never used Drashti:

- **One screen, nothing to find.** The playlist sits on the left with its headers. In the middle are what's live and what's next. The big buttons run along the bottom: Back, Next (the biggest, `primary`: since Session 26g the lamp's plum, lit from above, the brightest thing on the screen), Black-out, Logo, and Clear all. The header carries the lamp beside the name, and its light falls across the window, as in Pro Mode. After Clear all, that button turns into **Put it back** (`warning`).
- **Big targets.** The buttons are `xl` and `xxl` (64 and 96 px high), playlist rows are at least 56 px, and text is at least 16 px except the key hints.
- **States in words.** Black-out and Logo use the `live` variant when on, and their words change ("Black-out is on", "Logo is on"), read out once (no `aria-pressed` besides). The live playlist row has a LiveBadge.
- **Nothing that changes anything.** No editing, removing, importing, themes, screens, sound or backups. Don't add a control here that changes the library or the setup. The main process refuses those requests in Simple Mode anyway (`src/main/simple-mode.ts`).
- **Undo comes first.** Back undoes the last Next exactly, and Put it back undoes Clear all. Every new action in Simple Mode needs a way to undo it, in one press.

- **Which mode Drashti starts in** (Session 20, `startingMode` in `src/shared/mode.ts`): after an unexpected stop less than 3 hours before the start, the mode it was in; otherwise (a quit on purpose, or a stop 3 hours or more before, Session 21), Pro Mode, or Simple Mode with roles on. It is the same answer restart recovery uses (`recentStop` from `startupRecovery`): did the last run stop unexpectedly, within `RECOVERY_MAX_AGE_MS`?
- It fits 1280 × 720 with nothing scrolling, its way out at the top (`tests/e2e/simple-mode.spec.ts`).

## 11. The slide editor

The slide editor (`src/renderer/src/editor/`) covers the operator window, as Edit words does, so nothing behind it can be pressed by mistake. Along the top: its name, Undo and Redo, what can be added (Words, Shape, Picture or video), then Cancel and Save (the primary button; **Done** when nothing has changed). Below: the slides on the left, the slide in the middle, the inspector on the right.

- **The slide is drawn by the slide renderer**, scaled to fit, so what is seen is what the screens show. Selection is the `accent` colour: an outline round each selected element, square white handles to resize one and a round one above it to turn it, all a constant size on screen. Snapping guides are `guide` (a pink that shows on any slide), across the whole slide.
- **The keyboard.** The slide is one focus stop (`role="application"`, with its keys in its label): Tab chooses the next element and, after the last, moves on to the inspector, so keyboard users are never trapped. A polite live region says what is selected, where and how big. Esc steps back one stage at a time: typing, then the selection, then the editor (asking first when there are changes).
- **The inspector** groups its fields in titled sections (Selected, Place and size, Words, Shape, Picture/Video). Numbers change as they are typed; colours use the system picker. It says whether text styles go to the whole box or to the selected words.
- **Typing in place** shows a dashed `accent` outline round the box; the words keep their look while typed.
- **Several selected** keep their own outlines and get one dashed `accent` box round them all, with the same square handles and round handle, which act on them together (snapping as one); "2 elements selected" is read out. Copy, Cut and Paste are the Edit menu's (and their keys); "Copied 1 element." shows in the editor's note line.
- Questions use the usual alert dialogs: "Keep your changes to the slides of “…”?" (`KeepChangesDialog`: Save changes has the focus and Esc keeps editing; it says when the presentation is on the screens, because saving then changes them at once, and Keep editing has the focus then, so Enter never changes the screens by accident) and "Save over the other change?".
- It fits at 1280 × 720 with nothing cut off, and axe finds nothing serious, also while typing (`tests/e2e/editor.spec.ts`).

## 33. The logo and the app icon

- **The logo is the lamp's flame** (Session 19): a diya's flame with its bright core, over the lamp, on a deep plum tile (`build/icon.svg`). Drashti means sight: the flame is the light we see by, and the arti's light. Its colours (plum `#5e1b52` to `#2a0b27`, flame `#ff7a2f` to `#ffc23d`, core `#fff3c4`, lamp `#ffd36b` to `#ef9b33`) are the logo's own: they are not UI tokens. Since Diya (Session 26g) the window uses them in three places only, all drawn by `ui/Mark.tsx` from the icon's own paths and colours (`src/main/mark.test.ts` keeps them the same): **the mark** beside the name in the header, Simple Mode and the phone pages (small, so each part is filled flat in its gradient's middle colour, `middle` in `ui/Mark.tsx`); **the lamp lighting** once on the first start; and **the unlit lamp** (an outline in `faint`, with one wisp of smoke as it arrives) in an empty place. The lamp's light (`--lamp-*` in `app.css`) falls from the mark across the header (14%) and the window (10%), with a hairline under the header from flame to plum, and glows faintly behind an empty place's lamp: always a gradient on a surface, never a filled shape, so it is never taken for a warning, which is a filled amber box with an amber rail, an icon and words (`Notice`).
- **One source, drawn on Apple's grid:** a 1024 px canvas with the tile (`id="tile"`) at 100 to 924 and 185 px corners. The Mac's icons keep that margin; Windows' icons are filled 94% by the tile; the Home Screen icon for an iPhone or iPad is the tile edge to edge with square corners, because iOS rounds them itself. `pnpm icons` draws every size from the one source, with no separate drawing for small sizes, so the shapes must stay apart at 16 px: one flame, one core, one lamp, and a gap in the tile's colour between the flame and the lamp.
- **It reads at 16 px on a dark and a light Dock or taskbar.** Look at any change at 16, 32 and 128 px on both before committing it.
- **Never** the BAPS logo, a murti or a real picture, and nothing like ProPresenter's icon. Where it came from: `LICENSES/logo/`.

## 35. Repeat Slides and the transitions

- **The transitions are the slide layer's** (`src/renderer/src/render/transition-frame.ts`), for any slide or presentation: **Fade** (the dissolve: the new slide added light for light over the old, so nothing dips), **Fade through black** (black over the old slide, whole half-way, then going from over the new one: the background layer is covered too, so it is truly through black), **Slide** (in from the right as the old one goes out to the left, easing in and out, kept inside the screen) and **Slow zoom** (a fade, then the slide growing about its middle to 108% over its time up, or 20 s, run by the browser's compositor). The stage screen always cuts.

## 38. The Picture editor and sharpness
