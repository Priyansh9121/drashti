# Running a show

> Drashti is built independently for use at a BAPS mandir. It is not an official BAPS product.

Until 26 Nov 2026 this was the README's "Running a show" section. It moved here whole, word for word, so that the README is short enough for GitHub to show all of it on the repository's front page. It says how each part of a show works, and why.

- Names in quotes, such as "Backgrounds" or "Streaming and recording", are parts of this page or sections of the [README](../README.md); "above" and "below" mean there.
- The steps, chapter by chapter, are in the [Drashti Manual](manual/start.md).

The Manual keeps each tool in its chapter: [playlists, Find and M3U](manual/playlists.md#the-open-playlist), [playback, Resume and the time bar](manual/live.md#the-time-bar), [Audio and the levels](manual/audio.md), [the tools and QR downloads](manual/tools.md), [screens and shared show view](manual/screens.md), [streaming and YouTube](manual/stream.md), [appearance](manual/settings.md#appearance) and [reporting a problem](manual/help.md#report-a-problem). These are operating instructions; the sections below also describe how they work.

**Another app's window as the background** (Session 48, `src/main/live-window/`, `src/renderer/src/live-window/`). **Show a Window…** (in its section under LIVE and the Tools menu, Pro Mode; `operator/SetupTools.tsx`) lists other apps' open windows, by name with a small live picture: the main process lists only windows (`desktopCapturer`, types `window`), and leaves out any display and every one of Drashti's own (`sources.ts`); only a window it just offered can go up, through `ShowEngine.showWindow`, never an engine command (engine state 12: a background of kind `window`). Every screen's page on this computer (each output, the stream's page, the operator window's pictures, small) captures it once (`render/live-window.ts`) after asking the main process, whose capture handler then gives that page that window, once: no page names what it captures. It is fitted inside with black around it and silent; slides and everything above go over it, black-out and the Wallpaper (the logo until Session 55) cover it, Clear all and F3 take it off, Put it back brings it back. Its picture is kept about once a second: when the window closes or hides, the screens keep the last one and a notice in the window's flow says so (the main process looks every 2 s), with Clear the window. Stage screens say "Window on the screens", phones name it, nodes draw nothing for it, and restart recovery names it but never puts it back. On a Mac it needs Screen Recording (System Settings; ad hoc signing can make macOS ask again after an update). The tests use stand-in placeholder windows (`DRASHTI_TEST_WINDOW_STANDIN`): `tests/e2e/show-a-window.spec.ts`.

**A live stream's link as the background** (alpha.20, the playback set's slice 8; `src/main/live-link/`, `src/renderer/src/live-link/`, `render/LinkView.tsx`, `render/live-playback.ts`). **Show a Stream Link…** (its section under LIVE and the Tools menu, Pro Mode) checks the link as it is typed (`shared/live-link.ts`: an http or https link whose path ends in `.m3u8`, or a YouTube video's link as its Share button gives it; never a name and password, a file or another kind of address). A YouTube link is looked at with the bundled yt-dlp (`youtube-look.ts`: `--dump-single-json`, nothing of the user's, never signed in, the lowest priority, only while the stream is off air and not recording; `youtube-live.ts` takes its HLS stream of picture and sound together, H.264, at most 1080 lines). The playlist is read first (`fetch-playlist.ts`, `hls-playlist.ts`: straight to its server, no proxy, at most 1 MB in 10 s; a master playlist's H.264 variant at most 1080 lines; an ended or VOD playlist refused, so a finished stream is never received from its start). Main's own FFmpeg (`relay-args.ts`, `relay-process.ts`: below normal priority, never the lowest, which Windows starves while busy; `-protocol_whitelist http,https,tcp,tls,crypto`, `-c copy`, nothing decoded) copies it into a short local HLS of MPEG-TS segments in `Stream links/<id>/` in the data folder (about six kept; emptied at each start, its FFmpeg written down as a leftover, `system/leftovers.ts`), served by `drashti-media://live/<id>/<segment>/` (`media-protocol.ts`; never kept by a page). Once three segments are written the engine puts it up (`ShowEngine.showLink`; engine state 30: a background of kind `link`, put up by the main process alone, never by a command), taking the covers off as a video does; a cover going on takes it off. Chromium's own HLS player plays it (x47's probe: MPEG-TS on both systems), but cannot seek a live stream and gives each player its own time from its first segment (x54's probe), so a page joins it as Main says (`IPC.liveLink.join`: the segment its player starts at, given a playlist that starts there, and the engine's moment for that segment's first frame), holds that frame until then and plays from then at normal speed always (Chromium's live player gives up at any other: CI x54-1120-210932-7783), waiting a moment, paused, when ahead and joining again when it falls behind (its picture kept until the new player plays); the pages run on one clock. If it drops (no new segment for 6 s), LIVE's notice says Reconnecting…, the screens keep the last picture, and its FFmpeg is started again (1, 2, 4 s, then every 5 s), carrying the local playlist on; every page starts again together when it comes back (`restartLink`). After 20 s the engine puts the Wallpaper up itself (`linkDropped`), the link off under it; when its pictures come again it goes up again by itself, so long as the Wallpaper is still on, black-out off, no background, and the same slide and box as then. Taken off any way, its FFmpeg stops and its folder goes; Put it back starts the last one again. Drashti's own stream draws the Wallpaper over everything while one is up (`Scene.tsx`, `place="stream"`) and mixes none of its sound; a key and fill pair draws nothing of it; stage screens say "Stream link on the screens"; a phone names it ("A stream link: …") with Clear; nodes draw nothing of it; restart recovery names it but never puts it back. The log names the host only. A test run asks nothing of any address but 127.0.0.1 (the tests' live stand-in, `tests/e2e/live-stand-in.ts`; YouTube only through the fake yt-dlp): `tests/e2e/stream-link.spec.ts`.

**A video input as the background** (Session 50, `src/main/video-inputs/`, `src/renderer/src/video-inputs/`, `render/video-input.ts`). **Add a Video Input…**, in the Video inputs section under LIVE and in the Tools menu (Pro Mode), lists this computer's cameras and capture inputs by name with small live pictures (a capture card, or a video mixer's program through its USB output); the operator chooses one and names it (it offers "Input 1"; a name another input has is refused; 60 characters at most, and 20 inputs on a computer), with **Fit inside, with black around it** or **Fill the screen**, and **Add to Media** keeps it: nothing goes up from the dialog. Media's **Video inputs**, above the files, lists them, each with its own small live picture and a line saying how it sits or why it has no picture; one click puts one up through the engine's `showInput`, which looks it up in this computer's list by its id (a page never names a device): a background of kind `input` (engine state 12) that dissolves in over a fixed second (`INPUT_FADE_MS`, as the mandir's setup does), with the slide, the lower third, messages and the ticker over it. **Change** and **Delete** (asked first) sit at the row's end; the input up now cannot be deleted until it is cleared. The list is kept in `drashti-video-inputs.json` in the data folder, never in the library (a device's name belongs to its computer, and a backup restored on another must never name devices it does not have); naming, changing and deleting them is Pro Mode's. Each page that draws it on this computer (every output, the stream's page, LIVE and Media's small pictures) opens it once, by the device's name, video only and never a microphone; black-out and the Wallpaper (the logo until Session 55) covered it until Session 56, when LIVE came to show one thing at a time: they take it off as they take a video off, and it takes them off as it goes up; Clear all and F3 take it off, Put it back brings it back. It shows on LIVE, the hall's screens and the stream's Slides layout (YouTube sees what the hall sees); the Camera layout keeps the stream's own camera; stage screens say "Video input on the screens"; a phone names it ("Video input: Input 1") with Clear, and can never choose one in Media (a playlist's video input item it may start, below); nodes draw nothing for it, and the screens dashboard says so. No sound comes from it in the hall; a mixer's USB output can be the stream's sound input instead. When it stops (unplugged, or another app has it) the screens keep its last picture (`render/KeptVideo.tsx`, copied about once a second), a notice in the window's flow says so with **Clear the input**, and it comes back by itself with the input. After a restart it is put back while that input is in this computer's list; when the screens find no camera of that name (since Session 51 they have up to 20 s to say, not 8: `RESTORE_WAIT_MS`, as a screen's first frame after a crash took up to 8.1 s on CI's Mac), it comes off and the recovery notice says "The video input “Input 1” was not there after the restart, so it was not put back." On a Mac the camera is asked for only after Drashti has said why (**Continue**), and no page is given a camera until System Settings' Camera says allowed; a refusal shows the steps with **Open System Settings** (Privacy & Security › Camera). Windows says plainly when its camera privacy setting is off, with **Open Settings**. Simple Mode cannot choose one in Media, and can clear it. **In a playlist, and behind a slide** (Session 51, `inputNamed` in `src/shared/video-inputs.ts`): a row of Media's Video inputs goes into a playlist as the library's other rows do (**Add to playlist** under the chosen row, **Add to “…”** in its right-click menu, or dragged into the playlist), and the slide editor's picker for a slide's background lists **Video inputs on this computer**. Both keep the input's own name ("Input 1"), never its id: an input belongs to one computer while a library travels (a backup put back on another, a spare computer taking over), so each computer finds its own input of that name, in any letter case or Unicode form. Migration 47 builds `playlist_items` again with the kind `input` and an `input_name` column (every row and rowid kept, its indexes and the nodes' triggers made again); a slide's cue is a background cue with no file and `{"input": "Input 1"}` in its settings. The item's card says **Video input**, the middle shows its live picture, and a click or Next puts it up as the background over its fixed second and takes the slide off, as a picture or video item does; a slide whose cue names one puts that input up behind the slide, and the same input carries on. On a computer with no input of that name it is as a missing file is: the card says **Not on this computer**, the middle says why ("No video input called “Input 1” is on this computer, so Next goes past it."), starting it says "The video input “Input 1” is not on this computer", Next goes on past it, and a slide naming it leaves the background as it was, never black because of it, while LIVE says "Video input not on this computer: “Input 1”. The background stays as it was." Since Session 53 LIVE says it again for the slide restart recovery puts back, as it says a missing file again (`restoreAt` in `show-engine.ts` says `inputMissing` again; until then it was said only as the slide went up: `tests/e2e/video-input-items.spec.ts:319`, `src/main/engine/input-items.test.ts`). **Renamed** (Session 52, `carryInputName` in `src/main/db/input-renames.ts`): renaming an input in Media (its row's **Change**, its **Name**, **Save**) carries every playlist item and slide's background cue in the library that named it to the new name, in one write, before anyone is told (`VideoInputsService.update`, through its `renamed`): removed items, templates and playlists in the bin too, and the slides' copies kept for Undo (`Revisions.carryInput`), each name matched as `inputNamed` matches it (`namesInput`: any letter case or Unicode form, spaces at either end aside); a picture's or a video's cue, and a header with the same words, are left alone. What is up carries on (it follows the input by its id), Next finds it by the new name, and the phones and nodes see the playlists change. It is not on the library's Undo: renaming it back carries them back. When the library cannot be written the rename is refused and the input keeps its name ("The playlists and slides that name “Input 1” could not be changed, so it keeps its name. Try again."). Another computer still finds its own input by the name, so give a spare computer's input the same new name there (its own **Change**), or its item and slide say Not on this computer; a backup made before the rename still names the old one. Before, a rename left every item and slide that named the input on the old name, Not on this computer (`tests/e2e/video-input-items.spec.ts`; `src/main/db/input-renames.test.ts`). Since Session 53 the **Change** dialog says so, in a muted line under **Name** and **Fit**, the first of its muted lines: "Renaming it takes this computer’s playlist items and slides that use it to the new name. The other computer’s input needs the same new name." (since Session 54 "The other computer’s": "The spare computer’s" read oddly when renaming on the spare, as both run as Main; `RENAMING` in `src/renderer/src/video-inputs/words.ts`, shown by `VideoInputDialog.tsx`). It is there from the start while the dialog shows **Name**, not only once a new name is typed, so it is read before one is chosen and the line never makes the dialog grow or move as one is typed; it is the dialog's description (`aria-describedby`), so a screen reader says it as the dialog opens; it never shows in Add a Video Input…, nor while the camera is being asked for or was refused (`tests/e2e/video-input-items.spec.ts:359`, which also sees the dialog fit a 1024 × 645 window with Cancel and Save in reach, and `tests/e2e/video-input.spec.ts`; `src/renderer/src/video-inputs/words.test.ts`). A slide's thumbnail names the input behind it, marked where this computer has none of that name; Next's preview, the stage screens and a phone name it ("Video input: Input 1"; a phone may start the item, as any item), templates keep it, Repeat Slides refuses it, restart recovery keeps the playlist's place on it, and Simple Mode plays it through the playlist (its Next, or a click on the item), still never choosing one in Media (`tests/e2e/video-input-items.spec.ts`). If the mixer's program is Drashti's own picture, showing it makes a picture inside itself, over and over: put it up while the mixer shows a camera (the dialog says so). The tests use Chromium's fake camera, named "Placeholder input" (`DRASHTI_TEST_FAKE_DEVICES`, `DRASHTI_TEST_CAMERA_ACCESS`): `tests/e2e/video-input.spec.ts`.

**Picture in picture** (Session 50, `src/main/engine/pip.ts`, `src/main/pip/`, `render/PipLayerView.tsx`, `render/pip.ts`). **Picture in Picture…**, in its section under LIVE and in the Tools menu (Pro Mode), chooses what goes in a box on the audience screens (the slides, a video input, a picture or video from Media, or a window Show a Window… offers), its corner, its size (**Small**, **Medium** or **Large**: a quarter, a third or two fifths of the screen's width, always 16:9), a thin border, rounded corners, and how it comes on and goes off (a fade, a scale, or a slide in from its corner); nothing goes up until **Show in the box**. The engine's `layers.pip` holds it, its picture looked up by the main process and never taken from a page, and every screen works out its in, out, swap and move from the engine's clock, at every drawing since Session 51 (only opacity and transform move, as the lower thirds do; a clock jump draws the screen again only when the box changed). With the slides in it, the main picture shows the background, props, messages and the ticker without the slide, and **Swap** shows the slide full with the background in the box; with anything else in it, Swap puts it in the background's place and the background in the box. Since Session 52 the one picture that changes on the main picture (the box's own, or the slide for a box of the slides) dissolves into the one replacing it, opacity alone, on the Movement's curve as a slide's dissolve is (Calm smoothstep, Bold its ease in and out: `dissolveCurve` in `render/transition-frame.ts`), in the half of the swap in which the box moves the same way: coming onto the main picture as the box comes in again (the second half), going from it as the box goes out (the first) (`pipMainDissolve` and `pipMainOpacity` in `render/pip.ts`, painted by `usePipMainDissolve` in `PipLayerView.tsx`). Through that half both are drawn, the upper added light for light over the other (`plus-lighter`; `pipMainPlaces` marks the two, `MainSlot` in `Scene.tsx` draws them), so they add up to the whole picture at every moment: never a cut, never black, and nothing on the main picture shrinks, grows or slides, whatever the box's own move (a fade, a scale or a slide, as before). The black rectangle under a moving main picture is gone, so nothing in the swap depends on the screen: the hall's screens, the stream and a key and fill pair draw it alike. Session 51 gave that picture the box's own move (Scale grew or shrank it, Slide moved it towards the box's corner) over a black rectangle (none on a key and fill pair); before Session 51 the main picture cut from one to the other at the swap's middle. Under LIVE a strip names what is in the box with **Swap**, **Take off** and its settings, and the box itself can be dragged on the LIVE picture (let go near a corner, it goes into it; the dialog's corners do the same from the keyboard). Black-out and the Wallpaper (the logo until Session 55) cover it; Take off plays its going out (`pipOff`) and Clear all takes it off at once; Put it back brings it back; until Session 55 a Look could leave it out of a group (the **Picture in picture** layer in Screens' Look settings; a Look saved before shows it: `leftOut` in `src/shared/looks.ts`), and since Session 51 a key and fill group leaves it out unless its Look turns it on, in Looks saved before and since alike (kept as `turnedOn`; a group's defaults follow its role as it is now, `src/main/db/looks.ts`). The stream's Slides layout and a stage screen's audience picture box show it, and since Session 51 the Camera layout's full-frame moments too (a picture or video going live, or a picture slide): those are drawn by the hall's own Scene, with only the background, the slide and the box (`program/full-frame.ts`), so the box comes, swaps and goes there as on the hall's screens, while the props, the Lower Thirds panel's lower third and the messages stay the Camera layout's own, over it; the stream's own camera never has the box over it. Nodes draw a box of the slides or of media; a video input or a window in it is drawn on this computer's screens only. No sound comes from it (a video in it plays muted and loops, since Session 53 between its file's start and end points, in the box and swapped: see "Playback markers"). Its place and look are remembered in the library's settings (`setting.pip.place`, `src/main/pip/pip-places.ts`), as the operator saves them and as the box is moved. Restart recovery puts it back (since Session 53 a video from Media in it between its file's start and end points, where it would be), and names a window that was in it without putting that back. Since Session 51 a video input in it is checked as the background's is: one gone from this computer's list, or whose camera the screens do not find (they have up to 20 s to say), leaves the box off, and the notice says "The video input “Input 1” in the picture-in-picture box was not there after the restart, so the box was not put back." (`pipInputGone`; `tests/e2e/pip-input-recovery.spec.ts`); and Put it back (or Back) brings a box's input back as Media has it now, or not at all once it is deleted. A phone names it ("Picture in picture: …") with **Take off** (`POST /api/v1/clear/pip`) and can never set one up, swap or move it; Simple Mode can only take it off. `tests/e2e/pip.spec.ts`.

The first time Drashti starts, its welcome offers **Set Up Screens…** (afterwards: **View > Set Up Screens…**, on Windows press Alt for the menu bar, or **Set Up Screens…** in Screens; never in Simple Mode), which opens on its displays (Session 44). **Show each display's number on it** puts a big number across every display for five seconds (not the one the controls are on), so volunteers can see which output feeds which screens. For each display it asks its job, one click among **Audience**, **Stage** and **Not used** (as each display does now: since Session 55 displays set themselves as they are plugged in, so nothing is chosen for a free one), and a kirtan's languages on it; **Done** sets the screens there and then (Session 44). **More setup…** goes on to the sound output, with **Play a test tone** (a short beep through the audio player, on the output being tried, before anything is chosen), the stream (and the default theme until Session 55), and nothing changes until **Finish**. Done and Finish set it all at once: outputs join a group with their role and languages (a group whose screens all change the same way just changes, keeping its name; otherwise one named "Audience" or "Stage" is made), a display already in use keeps its screen and canvas, and **Not used** takes a display's output away; displays that are not connected are left alone; an output on the controls' display asks first, as in Screens. There is no test slide (Session 55): each screen shows what is up, or black until something is. Since Session 50 the wizard marks **The controls are on this display** wherever it offers that display (in Finish's summary too), and Done and Finish wait for **Keep these screens?** when they put an output on the controls' display (see "Keeping the controls reachable"; since Session 55 only then): gone back, the wizard is at its displays again, with the choices as made. On 29 Oct 2026 every Done asked, and its Esc (Go back) brought the wizard back to its displays, so Done, Esc, Done went round in a loop; a Done elsewhere now changes the screens at once. A screen that Audience or Stage switched off counts as in use there, as the wizard opens and as Keep these screens? goes (Session 51). **Skip this step** keeps that step's setting as it is, and closing the wizard keeps everything. **Show on** (Session 44) is gone with Session 55: a display plugged in is used by itself (see "Screens that set themselves" above). Drashti never changes a display's resolution or refresh rate.

1. A display plugged in is used at once (see "Screens that set themselves" above). To set one by hand, open **Screens** and give its display a job in one click (**Audience**, **Stage** or **Not used**), or add a group (for example "Main Hall") and press **Use this display** next to each display that feeds the audience. Set the canvas size and scaling if a screen needs something other than 1920 × 1080 fit. **Identify screens** shows each screen's name on it. The setup is saved and comes back on the next start. If a display is missing at startup, its screen says "Display not connected" and opens by itself when the display returns.
2. Open a playlist, or pick a presentation in the library (**Library**, at the top of the left column), and click a row to put it on the screens, or press Space or the right arrow. Since Session 55 a presentation's slides are rows under its name, in play order, each with its group's colour and the group's name on the first slide each time a group comes up (until then they were thumbnails in the slides area below the middle, sized by a slider).
3. Use the clear buttons along the bottom (F1 to F3 and F5 to F7 since Session 55, when F4 and F8 went with props and masks) and **Black-out** (B) at their right, with the **Wallpaper** (F12) beside it. A layer's clear is lit, with a dot, while that layer has something on the screens, and shows its icon alone while it has nothing (alpha.24).

**A screen's geometry** (x27, alpha.20; `src/shared/screen-geometry.ts`, `src/renderer/src/output/main.tsx`, `screens/AdjustPicture.tsx`, `screens/WidePicture.tsx`). PLAN §2's edge blend, corner-pin and output scaling, per screen, on the output window's whole picture: one box around everything the screen draws carries one CSS `matrix3d`, the zoom about the display's centre and the move first, then the homography that takes the window's corners to the four pinned ones (Heckbert's square-to-quadrilateral). Inside the canvas, over everything, the edge blend's ramps (`output/BlendRamps.tsx`: black, one exact alpha a canvas pixel, drawn once; the light a projector gives across the overlap is ½(2t)^curve, then 1 - ½(2(1 - t))^curve, so two projectors' light adds up to the picture's own, and the pixel is multiplied by that to the power 1/gamma) and, while **Adjust the picture…** is open, the grid (`output/GeometryGrid.tsx`, over the show, warped with it). With nothing set the box has no style and nothing more is drawn: the same page as before; the box is always there, so a change never mounts the show again (no blank frame, no video loaded again). Kept with the screen in the library (migration 85, `screens.geometry` as JSON, checked as it is read: corners that would fold the picture, a part outside the canvas or an overlap over half its part are refused), so it follows the display through a restart, another port and a change of job, Keep these screens? and Go back. A key and fill pair's key draws its fill's (`drawnGeometry`); a node's screens carry theirs from Main and the node keeps the last it was given (never the grid). The stream, LIVE, the projector cards, the stage display's audience picture and the phones never go through it. One wide picture (`makeWidePicture`): an audience group's two to four screens get the wide canvas (their widths added up, less the overlaps), each its part and the ramps over its inner edges; Separate pictures undoes it. The tests: `src/shared/screen-geometry.test.ts`, `src/main/outputs/screen-geometry-service.test.ts` and `tests/e2e/screen-geometry.spec.ts` (real pixels: a pinned corner where the maths puts it, the ramps' light adding up to the white's across the overlap, a key and fill pair aligned, the grid over a show that never blanks, the stream unwarped, and the dialog); the Performance check's animations scenario measures dissolve 800 and the deck's clicks on a warped hall screen (`perf-animations.ts`, rows ending "its screen’s picture pinned, zoomed and blended").

**Looks** (taken out in Session 55, Part 12: each group's languages are set on its card in Screens, `screens/GroupLanguages.tsx`, and kept where Drashti always kept them, in the library's first Look; what a group draws comes from its role, `roleLook` in `src/shared/looks.ts`: an audience, stage or stream group the background, the slide, picture in picture, messages and the ticker, slides as designed, a key and fill group the slide, as a lower third, and messages; every group moves Calm. The library keeps every Look, unread. What follows is how they were.) A Look says what every screen group shows: which layers its screens draw (background, slides, props, the picture-in-picture box since Session 50, messages, the ticker and the Masks layer; a Look saved before the box shows it, but in a key and fill group, which since Session 51 leaves it out until a Look turns it on), a kirtan's languages and their order, and whether slides are drawn as designed or as a lower third (the slide's words in a box along the bottom, as the stream's Camera layout draws them; a slide with no words but a picture is drawn as designed). Over a lower third, messages go along the top, as in the stream's Camera layout, and the words sit above the ticker, so nothing covers them. A lower third holds at most 6 lines (`LOWER_THIRD_MAX_LINES` in `src/shared/program.ts`, passages from books counted by the same); when the live slide has more where one is drawn now (a group's Look, or the stream's Camera and words), a note under LIVE says so: "Lower third shows 6 of 7 lines" (Sessions 31–36, SLD-8; `tests/e2e/lower-thirds.spec.ts`). One Look is live at a time. **Looks** in the right column switches it: every group changes in one change of the show, in step. In Screens, under **Looks**: **New Look**, **Duplicate**, rename, **Up**/**Down** and **Remove** (never the last one; removing the live one puts the first live); the group cards below show and change each group's settings in the Look chosen there. Drashti starts with the first Look after a clean quit, so a volunteer in Simple Mode never starts on last week's festival Look; after an unexpected stop the Look that was live comes back, and the recovery notice says so. Simple Mode keeps the live Look: the engine refuses switching it from anywhere, and the Looks cannot be changed. The upgrade to Looks (migration 20) made **Standard** from the groups as they were, so every screen shows exactly what it did. The stream group takes its languages and its Movement from a Look: its layout is the stream's own setting (YouTube's card in Screens and the Stream panel show the same one), and the Program keeps its own rules. **Movement** (Session 27): each group's screens move **Calm** (the default: slides fade in place) or **Bold** (the words may move, wipe and zoom; pictures and backgrounds only ever dissolve), chosen per group in each Look (Pro Mode; never in Simple Mode; the stage group always cuts). A transition's length is the slide's whatever the Movement, and a kind a Calm group cannot draw is drawn as Calm's dissolve there (`docs/design.md` section 40). The setup wizard's languages go into the live Look, and a group the wizard makes gets them in every Look. Phones (Remote devices) switch Looks in the remote's **More** tab, and scripts with `POST /api/v1/looks/{id}/live` (`docs/api.md`).

**Stage layouts.** A stage group shows the **Standard** stage screen (the slide and the next one, notes, the clock, running timers and the stage message, making room as they come and go), or a layout of boxes made in Drashti. **Making transliteration** (gone from the window with the Kirtan dialog in Session 55; what follows is how it was). Words read as they are said: the "a" each consonant carries is dropped at the end of a word ("ghar") and in the middle where it is not said ("bachpan", "akshardham"), and kept after a cluster ending in y, r, l, v or a nasal ("satya", "mitra", "dharma"). The two scripts differ where they are said differently: ઋ is "ru" and જ્ઞ "gn" in Gujarati, ऋ "ri" and ज्ञ "gy" in Hindi. **Kirtans and their languages** (since Session 55 the **Kirtan** button and its dialog are gone with the slides area: a kirtan's tracks stay, and what follows of the dialog is how it was). A kirtan's words come in up to four language tracks, Gujarati, Hindi, English (the meaning) and transliteration, slide by slide. They are not kept anywhere apart from the slides: each line on a slide has a language (its script decides Gujarati or Hindi; a Latin line is English or transliteration, as it was typed or imported), and a track is simply that language's lines (`src/shared/tracks.ts`). So Edit words, the slide editor, search and the screens always see the same words. **Kirtan**, above the slides, says whether the presentation is a kirtan, how many slides have each language, and has **Make it a kirtan** and **Not a kirtan** (each only adds or takes away the kirtan's details; the words never move; Undo puts it back). For a kirtan, **Edit words** has two views: **All words** (the plain text above, which adds and removes slides) and **By language**: every slide that plays, with its lines in one language, or in all of them side by side. A slide with no line in a language shows it as **Missing** (amber, dashed), not as an empty line; typing there adds it, in the look that language has elsewhere in the kirtan (or the theme's), after the languages that come before it on the slide. Changing a line keeps its look; taking a language's lines away closes up the rest. Only the lines that changed are rewritten, as one change for Undo. A Latin line changed in All words stays the language it was, so plain transliteration (with no accent marks) never turns into English. Copying a slide copies its lines in every language. In the slide editor, a text box with lines in several languages shows **Several** as its language.

**Editing slides.** **Edit slides** (beside **Edit words** until Session 55; since then in a presentation's header and the one right-click menu's **Edit**, or a double-click on a slide's row) opens the slide editor over the operator window, at the slide on the screens (or the one a search found). The slides are on the left, the slide being edited in the middle (drawn by the same renderer as the screens), and what is selected on the right.

Main now tells every window that draws the show, and each video that failed (a background, or one on a slide) loads again at a new address and plays from the engine's time (`watchGpuRestarts`, `src/renderer/src/render/media-attempts.ts`). A second stop within 10 minutes is told to the operator once (`src/main/gpu-stops.ts`). The method that proves it: Chromium's own `chrome://gpucrash`, loaded from the main process in a window that is never shown (`tests/e2e/gpu-crash.spec.ts`, with a 720p H.264 video; CI's virtual machines decode in software, so there it shows no harm, and the red was seen on a Mac with a GPU).

**The guides inside Drashti** (Sessions 31–36). **Help › Guides…** shows the operator's, presenter's, admin's and setup day's guides (`docs/operator-guide.md`, `docs/presenter-guide.md`, `docs/admin-guide.md`, `docs/setup-day.md`), in English, as they came with this Drashti, and, as Drashti's own screens, never naming the old presentation program (the `.pro6` and `.pro` files are named instead; `src/main/guides.test.ts`): `electron-builder.yml` ships the four and exactly the pictures they show beside the app (`resources/guides`; `src/main/guides.test.ts` keeps the list equal to what the guides show, and `scripts/check-installers.mjs` checks every installed app for them, byte for byte). The main process reads them (`src/main/guides.ts`) for the operator window only, through the bridge (`guides:list`, `guides:read`): they are never served to phones, and nothing is fetched (`tests/e2e/guides.spec.ts` stops and counts every request off the computer: none). They are drawn by Drashti's own small Markdown reader (`src/shared/markdown.ts`), element by element and never as HTML, so an address or a `<name>` is only words; a picture comes from the guides' folder only (`drashti-media://guide/<name>.png`, a name a guide shows), with its size read from its PNG header so its room is kept before it loads. A search finds the passages holding every word typed (each the start of a word, as the library's search folds them), by section; a result opens its guide there, marked. Up to three curated answers come first (`src/shared/guide-answers.ts`: 28 questions (the forgotten admin PIN's went in Session 54) asked as "When …, what do I do?", each answered by one passage, shown in its guide's own words, and offered only where that guide can be read): the answers holding every word typed, the fewest other words first, so an answer's own words always find it first (`src/main/guide-answers.test.ts` resolves each to exactly one passage, never a heading and never another answer's, and searches each by its question and by its passage's first words). Simple Mode sees the operator's and the presenter's guides only (the admin's and the setup day's are about setting Drashti up, which is Pro Mode's), and the main process refuses the other two there. It is a dialog like the keys sheet: Esc closes it, and the show's keys wait while it is open.

## Caption corrections (alpha.22)

While the speaker's language has a model of its own (from the offline model pack), **File › Live Captions…** (Pro Mode) shows the phrase on the screens now, provisional or settled, and the operator can correct or dismiss a settled phrase (`src/main/speech/caption-coordinator.ts`, Codex x90's; `src/main/speech/caption-flow.ts` feeds it from the speech hub). Session and revision checks refuse late edits. Only the current and previous phrase stay in memory. Audience routing, video-caption priority and the two-line band apply. Nothing is added to a transcript or the sound draft. The translation interface is in `src/shared/caption-coordinator.ts`; since alpha.24 live translation fills it (below).

## Translated live captions (alpha.24)

**Captions in** (Pro Mode, Trial, off until chosen; the owner's QA24-CAPLANG-2) translates live captions into Gujarati, Hindi or English on this computer, a whole phrase at a time. It uses the translation pack's IndicTrans2 engine, the one Prepare translations uses, in a greedy copy of its own below the show (`src/main/speech/live-translation.ts`, through the coordinator's translation port). Spoken times become digits first (`src/shared/spoken-times.ts`). The audience screens and the stream show the translation only, long ones as two-line pages; the stage screens keep the speaker's own words. A phrase whose translation is late is skipped and counted, never shown late, and captions never fall back to the speaker's language by themselves. The setting and the dialog's status are in `src/shared/caption-translation.ts`; the dialog's field is `src/renderer/src/captions/CaptionTargetField.tsx`, and each caption band says what it shows (`data-kind="translation"`, `data-page="n/of"`). Phones, the API and output nodes still get no live captions.
